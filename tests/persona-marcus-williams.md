# Marcus Williams — Workshop Journey

**Background:** Senior Law Partner, 41. Identity built on reputation and proving himself from poverty. Wound: built the wrong life. Purpose trajectory: fatherless young men.

---

## Identity Arc

### I-1. The Honest Inventory
*Where do I actually derive my worth right now?*

**Selected as threatening if removed:**
My reputation · My status or influence · My usefulness to other people · My competence or expertise · My sense of being needed

**Which one would be hardest to lose?**
My reputation. I grew up with nothing. The name I have built is the only thing that separates me from what I came from.

**If those were taken away, would you still be you?**
Someone who worked hard. But I am not sure if that is a person or just a habit.

**What does this reveal?**
That I have been running from something, not toward something. The career was escape dressed up as ambition.

**What would still be true of you before God?**
Someone made in the image of God. A son. But that feels like theology, not reality.

**What part of you is most resistant to being loved without earning it?**
Rest. Receiving. Anything that looks like slowing down, because slowing down feels like sliding back.

---

### I-4. Lies & True Names
*What false stories do I carry — and what does God say instead?*

**Write the exact sentence you hear:**
"You are a fraud and one day everyone will see it."

**Whose voice taught you that lie?**
No single voice. It is the absence of a voice. My father left when I was seven. The lie grew in the silence.

**What does it cost you?**
I overwork. I over-prove. I cannot receive a compliment without deflecting it. My wife says I am impossible to celebrate.

**Describe a recent incident:**
I won a significant case last quarter. The firm threw a dinner. I spent the whole evening thinking about the cases I might lose next year.

**What true name are you least able to receive?**
"Son." That is the name I cannot receive. Every other name — attorney, partner, provider — I can hold. That one breaks something.

---

### I-5. The Shaped Story
*What experiences, wounds, and graces have most formed me?*

**Describe the terrain:**
A long steep climb out of a valley I was born into, with no map and no guide. I reached the summit other people told me to aim for. It was not where I expected to feel what I expected to feel.

**Where have you been wounded in ways that still shape your instincts?**
The belief that I must justify my existence by what I produce for others. It is not generosity — it is a transaction. I give so that I cannot be sent away.

**Which graces have you minimised?**
My mother. She worked three jobs and still made Sunday dinner a ceremony. Grace was at that table every week and I treated it as ordinary.

**What part of your story do you avoid?**
The moment I realised I had become successful and was not happy. I drove past my old neighbourhood and felt nothing. I had expected pride. I felt empty.

**What would someone who loves you say?**
That the destination I was given was not mine. That the men I could have become were not the ones I chose to model. That there is still time.

---

### I-6. Repentance & Remaking
*What am I turning from — and who am I becoming?*

**What are you turning from?**
The belief that I owe the world full output at all times as proof I deserve to be here.

**What is the cost of keeping it?**
Everything the work was supposed to be for. My wife. The boys on Saturday mornings. The life I claimed I was building all this for.

**What act of obedience would prove this is real?**
Saying no to one corporate case this quarter without feeling I have to justify it.

**What must stop and what must begin?**
Stop: checking billing hours as a measure of my worth. Begin: treating Saturday morning as non-negotiable rather than optional.

**What support would help?**
Telling Denise what I have written here. She has been waiting for this conversation.

## Purpose Arc

### P-1. Values & Convictions
*What do I consistently choose when it costs me something?*

**Your ranking:**
Loyalty → Truth → Control → Approval → Peace → Comfort

**Which rises to the top in real conflict?**
Loyalty. I will take a case I cannot win for a client who cannot pay if they have been faithful. That has cost me more than I can calculate.

**Which looks good in theory but loses in practice?**
Peace. I say I want it. I manufacture conflict to avoid the silence where I would have to hear myself.

**Which has cost you something real?**
Truth. I told a senior partner his strategy was wrong in a partners meeting. He was wrong. I knew it would cost me. I said it anyway.

**What do you protect when pressure rises?**
The people I have made commitments to. Once I am in, I am in.

**What do you say no to?**
Cases where I think the client is lying to me. I do not take them regardless of the fee.

---

### P-2. How You're Made
*What has God built into me that energises rather than depletes?*

**What sits in the overlap?**
Listening in the gap — hearing what is not being said. I have always been able to sit with someone who has no voice in a room and help them find one. That is where talent, skill, and something I can only call calling converge.

**What kinds of work leave you more alive?**
The mentoring sessions on Saturday mornings. Every single time. I go in tired and come out with more energy than I arrived with.

**What do people ask you for?**
To translate complex systems for people who feel excluded from them. The law. The firm. The expectation. I make it readable.

**When do you lose track of time?**
Two Saturdays ago. A seventeen-year-old asked me what I wished someone had told me at his age. We talked for two hours. I forgot to check my phone.

**What drains you even when you are good at it?**
Corporate mergers. I am good at them. They empty me completely. I do not understand why I still take them.

---

### P-3. What Breaks Your Heart
*What injustice or need keeps finding me?*

**What keeps drawing your attention?**
Boys growing up without fathers. Without a map. Without anyone who looks like them and has stayed. I see myself at fourteen in every one of them.

**What do others seem to miss?**
How little it takes to change the trajectory. One consistent adult. One person who turns up. Nobody talks about that. They talk about policy.

**Describe a recent moment:**
A boy at the mentoring programme told me last month that I am the only man who has come back every week. He said it quietly, like it was ordinary. It destroyed me.

**What do you keep carrying?**
His face. The way he said it without drama. That will be with me for the rest of my life.

**What feels personal?**
I am not helping an abstract cause. I am going back for the version of myself that nobody went back for.

---

### P-4. The Convergence
*Where do my story, gifts, and burden point together?*

> **Prior answers brought forward:**
> - *Your life's lesson (I-5):* "That the destination I was given was not mine. That there is still time."
> - *Your tested value (P-1):* "Truth. I told a senior partner his strategy was wrong…"
> - *Your gift overlap (P-2):* "Listening in the gap… sitting with someone who has no voice and helping them find one."
> - *What you carry (P-3):* "His face. The way he said it without drama. That will be with me for the rest of my life."

**Where do story, gifts, and burden overlap?**
The story, the skill, the wound, and the work all point to the same place: young men without fathers who need someone to turn up, translate the system, and not leave. I have every tool I need. I have been using it for other people's purposes.

**What is supported by Scripture?**
"A father to the fatherless." Psalm 68:5. And the parable of the prodigal — not the son's return, the father who ran. I want to be the one who runs.

**Who could confirm or challenge this?**
My wife has been saying this for five years. My mother said it once and never repeated it because I changed the subject. The boys at the programme say it without knowing they are saying it.

**What fruit would you expect?**
Boys who know how to stay. Men who become the father they did not have. That is the only result that matters to me.

**If you named your calling in one sentence:**
I am called to be the consistent presence for fatherless young men that I never had — and to build structures that make that presence scalable.

---

### P-5. Community & Discernment
*How are my gifts and calling confirmed in the body?*

**Who knows you well enough?**
My wife Denise. Pastor Jerome. And David, who runs the mentoring programme and has watched me for three years.

**Where have others named strengths you overlooked?**
David said six months ago: "Marcus, the law pays you. This is where you are alive." I have been turning that over ever since.

**What counsel do you resist most?**
Anyone who tells me to leave the firm entirely. I hear it as reckless. But I am increasingly unsure if the caution is wisdom or fear.

**What has community already shown you?**
The boys keep coming back. That is the confirmation I keep dismissing because I do not trust good things to stay.

**What would confirmation look like?**
If I restructured my practice to do this work at scale and the boys still came, and I still felt what I feel on Saturday mornings — that would be enough.

---

### P-6. What's Mine to Do
*What is the one obedient step I'm being invited into?*

**The next faithful step:**
Have the conversation with my firm about reducing billable hours by twenty percent to formalise the mentoring work.

**What can you do in seven days?**
Book the meeting with the managing partner by Friday. I have been preparing for this conversation for a year.

**What makes this hard to start?**
I do not know who I am if I am not at full output at the firm. That is the honest answer.

**What support do you need?**
Denise. And Jerome. I need to tell them both I am doing this so I cannot retreat.
