# Priya Sharma — Workshop Journey

**Background:** Independent consultant, 52. Identity collapsed into usefulness and being needed. Wound: lost herself in roles for twenty years. Purpose trajectory: women in leadership finding their voice.

---

## Identity Arc

### I-1. The Honest Inventory
*Where do I actually derive my worth right now?*

**Selected as threatening if removed:**
My usefulness to other people · My relationships · My sense of being needed · My creativity or skill · My competence or expertise

**Which one would be hardest to lose?**
Being needed. When I am not needed I become invisible — to others and to myself.

**If those were taken away, would you still be you?**
Someone. But I honestly am not sure what that someone is apart from the helping.

**What does this reveal?**
That I have confused love with usefulness for most of my life. I give to be kept.

**What would still be true of you before God?**
Made in His image. Loved. But I only believe that for other people. For myself I always add a condition.

**What part of you is most resistant?**
Receiving care without giving something back. It feels dangerous. Like it could be withdrawn.

---

### I-2. The Divine Signature
*Who does God say I am before I do anything?*

**Where do you see yourself at the banquet?**
Moving around. Refilling glasses. Checking that everyone has what they need. Not sitting.

**Do you feel like a guest or a worker?**
A worker who has been invited to sit down and does not know how. Sitting feels like an imposition.

**When you hear "beloved" — what in you resists?**
The sense that I have not done enough to warrant it yet. That the invitation is provisional.

**What do you still act as if you have to prove?**
That I am worth the space I take up. That presence is not enough — I have to be useful in it.

**What changes when you remember you are already known?**
I might be able to receive from people instead of just giving. That is a frightening thought.

---

### I-3. The Unshakable Ground
*What does it mean that God is near by promise, not feeling?*

**Describe your image:**
God is present but I am always slightly turned away, arranging things for other people. He is there. I am busy.

**What do you do when you cannot feel God near?**
I help someone. Helping makes me feel functional, and functional feels close to faithful. I know they are not the same thing.

**What practices steady you?**
Walking in the early morning before I am needed by anyone. It is the only hour that does not have a recipient.

**Describe a season when feeling failed you:**
After my marriage ended. I had been so busy holding everyone else together that I had not noticed I had no interior life of my own. When everything fell quiet I found I had no practice for silence.

**What promise do you need to stand on?**
That He holds me when I am not holding anyone. That His nearness is not conditional on my function.

---

### I-4. Lies & True Names
*What false stories do I carry — and what does God say instead?*

**Write the exact sentence:**
"You are only as loveable as you are useful."

**Whose voice?**
My mother, indirectly. She modelled sacrifice so completely that I learned love and service were the same thing.

**What does it cost you?**
My marriage. My sense of self. Twenty years of being profoundly useful to everyone around me and unknown to myself.

**Describe a recent incident:**
My client cancelled a project last month and I felt personally abandoned. Not professionally frustrated — abandoned. I had to sit with how disproportionate that was.

**What true name are you least able to receive?**
"Known." Not useful, not capable — simply known. I cannot receive that without needing to earn it first.

---

### I-5. The Shaped Story
*What experiences, wounds, and graces have most formed me?*

**Describe the terrain:**
A long flat plain with sudden sinkholes. The sinkhole at 47 when my marriage ended. I had been helping everyone across the terrain and nobody had noticed I was disappearing into it.

**Where have you been wounded?**
I do not know how to be without a function. When my marriage ended and the children were older and the consulting work slowed down, I did not know what was left of me. That terrified me more than any professional failure.

**Which graces have you minimised?**
My friend Rekha, who kept showing up even when I had nothing to give her. She did not need me to be useful. I have never known what to do with that and I think it is the most important thing in my life.

**What part of your story do you avoid?**
The years when I was performing wellness while privately falling apart. I coached leaders through transitions while going through my own dissolution. The gap between what I projected and what I experienced was enormous.

**What would someone who loves you say?**
That I am not the shape I take for other people. That there is a self underneath the service that is worth knowing. And that the wound that nearly destroyed me is now the clearest signpost I have.

---

### I-6. Repentance & Remaking
*What am I turning from — and who am I becoming?*

**What are you turning from?**
Performing availability. Being reachable at all hours as proof of my worth. I am turning from the belief that my value is measured by my response time.

**What is the cost of keeping it?**
I am training people to need me rather than to find their own ground. That is not helping — it is dependency management disguised as service.

**What act of obedience would prove this is real?**
Turning my phone off between 7pm and 7am and not apologising for it.

**What must stop and what must begin?**
Stop: answering messages immediately as though delay means abandonment. Begin: one hour a week that belongs only to me with no agenda and no recipient.

**What support would help?**
Telling Rekha. She will not let me slip back. She never has.

## Purpose Arc

### P-1. Values & Convictions
*What do I consistently choose when it costs me something?*

**Your ranking:**
Loyalty → Peace → Truth → Approval → Comfort → Control

**Which rises to the top in real conflict?**
Loyalty. I stay longer than is healthy. I give more than is sustainable. I do it because I believe in showing up.

**Which looks good in theory but loses in practice?**
Truth. I teach it and I am frightened of it when it is about me. I deflect self-disclosure while facilitating it in others.

**Which has cost you something real?**
Peace — I left a long-term client relationship because the culture was damaging the people I was hired to help. I lost significant revenue. I have no regrets.

**What do you protect?**
The safety of the people in the room. Non-negotiable in every facilitation.

**What do you say no to?**
Work that requires me to help organisations look good while treating their people badly. I have turned away substantial contracts for this.

---

### P-2. How You're Made
*What has God built into me that energises rather than depletes?*

**What sits in the overlap?**
Holding space. Creating conditions where people — especially women — can hear themselves think without performing. I do not teach this skill; I embody it because I needed it so badly myself.

**What kinds of work leave you more alive?**
The moment a woman in a room I am facilitating stops performing and says something true. That moment never stops moving me.

**What do people ask you for?**
To be present in a way that gives permission. People say I make rooms feel safe. I think I make them feel seen.

**When do you lose track of time?**
Last Thursday. A two-hour session with six women executives. Every single person said something they had never said to a colleague before. I drove home not knowing what time it was.

**What drains you even when you are good at it?**
Strategy documents. I can produce them. They cost me something real and give me nothing back.

---

### P-3. What Breaks Your Heart
*What injustice or need keeps finding me?*

**What keeps drawing your attention?**
Women in senior leadership who are succeeding by every external measure and privately disappearing. I was them for twenty years. I cannot unsee them.

**What do others miss?**
That the language of resilience and high performance is being used to ask women to endure conditions that would not be tolerated elsewhere. I sit in rooms where this is normalised and I cannot stay silent.

**Describe a recent moment:**
A CEO told me in a session last year: "I do not know who I am outside of this role." She said it as though it were a confession. She expected me to fix it quickly and move on. I stayed with it for twenty minutes. She cried. She said no one had ever stayed with that.

**What do you keep carrying?**
That moment. And the weight of how many women have said the same thing in different words.

**What feels personal?**
I am not serving a cause. I am serving the woman I was at 47, sitting in my car not knowing who I was. This is that personal.

---

### P-4. The Convergence
*Where do my story, gifts, and burden point together?*

> **Prior answers brought forward:**
> - *Your life's lesson (I-5):* "That I am not the shape I take for other people. That there is a self underneath the service that is worth knowing. And that the wound that nearly destroyed me is now the clearest signpost I have."
> - *Your tested value (P-1):* "Peace — I left a long-term client because the culture was damaging the people I was hired to help."
> - *Your gift overlap (P-2):* "Holding space… creating conditions where people can hear themselves think without performing."
> - *What you carry (P-3):* "The weight of how many women have said the same thing in different words."

**Where do story, gifts, and burden overlap?**
My story of losing and finding myself is the exact terrain I help other women navigate. My skill in holding space is the tool. My burden for women who are succeeding and dissolving simultaneously is the fuel. It is the same thing pointing from every direction.

**What is supported by Scripture?**
"You are fearfully and wonderfully made." Psalm 139. And Mary at the feet of Jesus — the one who chose presence over performance, and was not corrected for it.

**Who could confirm or challenge this?**
Rekha said it years ago without knowing she was saying it: "You are the person I would send any woman to who has lost herself." I wrote it down. I have not known what to do with it until now.

**What fruit would you expect?**
Women who can name themselves without a title. Who lead from identity rather than fear. Who can receive as well as give.

**If you named your calling in one sentence:**
I am called to help women in leadership find and inhabit their own identity — so they lead from wholeness rather than performance.

---

### P-5. Community & Discernment
*How are my gifts and calling confirmed in the body?*

**Who knows you well enough?**
Rekha. My therapist Dr Okafor. And the cohort of women I trained with five years ago who have watched this emerge in me.

**Where have others named strengths you overlooked?**
The cohort said last year: "You do not just coach — you do something we do not have a word for. You make the room honest." That is the clearest external mirror I have.

**What counsel do you resist most?**
People who tell me to narrow my offer. I hear it as "shrink yourself." I need to learn the difference between faithful focus and diminishment.

**What has community already shown you?**
Every woman who has come back. Every referral from someone who said "I do not know what you did but I need to send my colleague to you." That is community discerning before I did.

**What would confirmation look like?**
If I built a programme specifically for this work and women came and left knowing themselves better — that would be all the confirmation I need.

---

### P-6. What's Mine to Do
*What is the one obedient step I'm being invited into?*

**The next faithful step:**
Name this work properly. Stop calling it consulting and start calling it what it is: identity accompaniment for women in leadership.

**What can you do in seven days?**
Rewrite the one-page description of my practice this week. New language. Honest framing. Send it to Rekha before Sunday.

**What makes this hard to start?**
Naming something makes it real and therefore possible to fail at. Vague is safer. But I am done with safe.

**What support do you need?**
Rekha. Dr Okafor. And the woman I mentor who has been asking me to do this for two years.
