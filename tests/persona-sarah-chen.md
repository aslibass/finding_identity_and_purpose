# Sarah Chen — Workshop Journey

**Background:** Chief Operating Officer, 48. Identity wrapped in competence and control. Father whose approval never came. Purpose trajectory: healthcare equity.

---

## Identity Arc

### I-1. The Honest Inventory
*Where do I actually derive my worth right now?*

**Selected as threatening if removed:**
My job or main role · My ability to do something I am known for · My reputation · My competence or expertise · My productivity · My status or influence

**Which one would be hardest to lose?**
My competence. Without it I genuinely do not know who I am. The work is what remains when everything else is removed.

**If those were taken away, would you still be you?**
Very little, honestly. That is the answer I have been avoiding.

**What does this reveal about where you have been locating your worth?**
That I have built my identity entirely on what I produce. I am a function, not a person, in my own eyes.

**If every title were removed, what would still be true of you before God?**
Someone He made on purpose. But I cannot feel that when I am not performing.

**What part of you is most resistant to being loved without earning it?**
The part that thinks love has to be earned. It is the deepest groove in me.

---

### I-2. The Divine Signature
*Who does God say I am before I do anything?*

**Where do you see yourself at the banquet?**
Near the back, close to the door. Near enough to be seen as attending. Far enough to leave if needed.

**Do you feel like a guest, a child, a worker?**
A worker who has been given a guest seat but still feels she should be clearing the plates.

**When you hear words like "beloved" — what in you resists them?**
The word beloved. It feels unearned. I hear it and immediately think: not yet.

**What do you still act as if you have to prove?**
That I deserve to take up space. That my presence without output is worth something.

**What changes when you remember you are already known?**
I would stop apologising for existing when I am not being useful. I would rest without guilt.

---

### I-3. The Unshakable Ground
*What does it mean that God is near by promise, not feeling?*

**Describe your image:**
God is above and ahead, facing away. I am behind, trying to keep up. The gap never closes, but I keep running.

**What do you do when you cannot feel God near?**
I work harder. I assume the silence means I have done something wrong. I interpret it as withdrawal, not waiting.

**What practices actually steady you?**
Early morning. When the house is quiet before the day makes demands. It is the only time I am not performing.

**Describe a season when feeling failed you:**
After my father died. Nothing felt true. I kept showing up to church but I was hollow. What held me was a verse my mother had written in the front of my Bible: "He will never leave you nor forsake you." I held onto the words when the feeling was gone.

**What promise do you most need to stand on without proof today?**
"I have called you by name, you are mine." I need to believe that is true before I have proved anything.

---

### I-4. Lies & True Names
*What false stories do I carry — and what does God say instead?*

**Write the exact sentence you hear:**
"You are only as good as your last result."

**Whose voice taught you that lie?**
My father. He was not cruel — he just never said it was enough. He always asked what was next.

**What does it cost you?**
It costs me rest. Relationships. The ability to receive. My husband says I am always preparing for a performance review that never ends.

**Describe a recent incident that triggered it:**
Last month I received a standing ovation from our board. I went home and lay awake listing what I should have said better. I could not receive it.

**What true name are you least able to receive?**
"Beloved daughter." It sounds true for other people. For me it still feels conditional.

---

### I-5. The Shaped Story
*What experiences, wounds, and graces have most formed me?*

**Describe the terrain of your story:**
Long plateaus of achievement at altitude — the mountains are real, I earned them. But every valley came without warning and I was not equipped for any of them. My father's death. My marriage nearly ending at 42. Each time I climbed back out by working harder.

**Where have you been wounded in ways that still shape your instincts?**
The belief that asking for help is weakness. I learned to be self-sufficient so early that dependence now feels like failure.

**Which graces have you minimised but keep reappearing?**
My daughter. She did not care what I achieved. She just wanted me to be home. I minimised that for years. Now I see it was the most honest mirror I had.

**What part of your story do you avoid telling?**
The years in my 30s when I missed things I cannot get back. I can explain everything else. I cannot explain those years.

**What would someone who loves you say your life has been trying to teach you?**
That I am not the sum of my outputs. That the people who loved me were not grading me. That I built a cage and called it a career.

---

### I-6. Repentance & Remaking
*What am I turning from — and who am I becoming?*

**What are you being asked to turn away from?**
The habit of working through every weekend. It is not ambition any more — it is avoidance. I am turning from the lie that rest is irresponsible.

**What is the cost of keeping the old pattern?**
I am afraid that if I stop, I will have to feel what I have been outrunning. That terrifies me more than failure.

**What small act of obedience would prove this is real?**
Taking one Sunday completely off this month. Not a half-day. A full day. That would be proof.

**What must stop and what must begin?**
Stop: answering emails after 7pm. Begin: thirty minutes on Saturday where I sit with nothing to produce.

**What support would help you follow through?**
I need to tell my husband what I am doing. He has been asking me to do exactly this for three years.

## Purpose Arc

### P-1. Values & Convictions
*What do I consistently choose when it costs me something?*

**Your ranking (what you protect under pressure):**
Control → Truth → Loyalty → Approval → Peace → Comfort

**Which value rises to the top in real conflict?**
Control. When I am under pressure I move to reduce uncertainty. I see now this is not a strength — it is a reflex.

**Which looks good in theory but loses in practice?**
Peace. I say I want it. I do not choose it. I choose certainty instead.

**Which has cost you something real?**
Truth. Last year I told our board a project was failing when everyone expected a recovery story. It cost me political capital. I would do it again.

**What do you protect when pressure rises?**
Control and truth. The first is fear-based. The second is actual conviction. I am starting to tell the difference.

**What do you say no to because of what you believe?**
Cutting corners on patient data integrity. Non-negotiable since I started.

---

### P-2. How You're Made
*What has God built into me that energises rather than depletes?*

**What sits in the overlap of talent, skill, and passion?**
Systems that serve people. Operational complexity that has a human on the other end. I am most alive when I am making something work that was failing the people it was supposed to help.

**What kinds of work leave you more alive?**
Fixing broken processes in clinical settings. Walking a ward and seeing flow where there was chaos.

**What do people consistently ask you for?**
To make sense of complexity. To tell the truth about what is not working. To stabilise things that are in crisis.

**When do you lose track of time?**
Last year, redesigning our patient discharge pathway. I lost three hours and felt nothing but clarity.

**What drains you even when you are good at it?**
Politics. Protecting decisions I know are wrong to maintain relationships. That hollows me out every time.

---

### P-3. What Breaks Your Heart
*What injustice or need keeps finding me?*

**What pain keeps drawing your attention?**
The gap in healthcare outcomes between postcodes. Same disease, different zip code, different survival rate. I see it in our data every week and it is not abstract to me.

**What do others seem to miss?**
That this is accepted as normal. People shrug. I cannot shrug.

**Describe a recent moment when it hit you hard:**
A patient in our lowest-income service area waited fourteen weeks for a procedure a patient in our premium service received in ten days. Same condition. Different address. I could not sleep that night.

**What do you keep carrying in memory?**
Her name was on the report. I remember her name. I do not remember the patients who got fast access.

**What feels personal, not abstract?**
My mother was that patient once. Before I had the knowledge or the position to change anything.

**Where do story, gifts, and burden overlap?**
Everything points to systemic healthcare equity. My story — my mother, my drive, my guilt — my skill in operations, my inability to ignore injustice, my value of truth over comfort. It is all the same thing.

**What is supported by Scripture?**
"Let justice roll down like water." Amos 5:24. And the parable of the Good Samaritan — he did not just feel compassion, he reorganised his journey around the need.

**Who in your community could confirm or challenge this?**
My colleague Dr Adeyemi has been saying this about me for two years. She said: "You have the skills, Sarah. You are just afraid to name it." She was right.

**What fruit would you expect?**
Measurable reduction in outcome disparity. And women in operations who see that this kind of leadership is possible.

**If you named your calling in one sentence:**
I am called to use operational excellence to dismantle the structural inequalities that keep people from the care they deserve.

*How are my gifts and calling confirmed in the body?*

**Who knows you well enough to confirm or challenge this?**
Dr Adeyemi. My husband James. And my mentor Carolyn, who led a health system for twenty years without losing her soul.

**Where have others named strengths you overlooked?**
James said years ago: "You are not just good at your job. You are good for the people your job is supposed to serve." I dismissed it. I should not have.

**What kind of counsel do you resist most?**
Counsel that tells me to slow down. I hear it as criticism. It is usually care.

**What has community already shown you?**
My team last year unanimously asked me to lead a new equity initiative. That was community speaking before I named the calling.

**What would confirmation look like?**
Seeing the patient outcomes data move. And feeling no gap between what I do at work and what I believe.
