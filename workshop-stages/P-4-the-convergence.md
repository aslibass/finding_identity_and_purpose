# P-4. The Convergence

## Invitation
Where do my story, gifts, and burden point together?

## What we are trying to surface
- The overlap between formed story, natural strength, and deep concern
- The shape of a calling that is more than preference
- Whether the participant can synthesize without forcing it

## Questions tab format

- Present each core question with multiple-choice options where possible.
- Include `None of these fit me yet` and `Other (my own words)` in each set.
- After selection, ask for one short concrete example before moving on.

## Exercise: Test the convergence

Ask the participant to test the emerging pattern against Scripture, community, and observable fruit.

### Exercise tab instructions

1. Write one short line under each heading: Scripture, Community, Fruit.
2. Continue only when all three headings are filled.

### Visual example

Scripture: Serves others, builds up the body.

Community: Two trusted people confirm this pattern.

Fruit: Consistent obedience, not short-term excitement.

## Core questions

### Q1. Where do your story, gifts, and burden overlap most clearly?

Options:
- Caring for people in pain with clarity and presence
- Teaching and forming people in truth
- Building communities of belonging and accountability
- Leading practical systems that support people well
- Guiding discernment and decision-making
- Integrating creativity with spiritual formation
- None of these fit me yet
- Other (my own words)

After selecting, write one recent example (1-3 sentences).

### Q2. What keeps appearing across your answers so far?

Options:
- A repeated people group
- A repeated kind of problem
- A repeated kind of contribution
- A repeated warning not to ignore this
- A repeated joy when serving this way
- A repeated call to repentance and courage
- None of these fit me yet
- Other (my own words)

After selecting, write one recent example (1-3 sentences).

### Q3. What would feel like a faithful next step, not just a good idea?

Options:
- Start a small pilot in current context
- Ask for discernment from trusted mentors
- Commit to one concrete weekly action
- Stop one competing commitment
- Seek training for this direction
- Test the call through service in community
- None of these fit me yet
- Other (my own words)

After selecting, write one recent example (1-3 sentences).

### Q4. What pattern seems too persistent to ignore?

Options:
- People keep coming to me with similar needs
- I keep returning to the same burden
- I keep seeing fruit in one type of service
- I keep feeling convicted about one direction
- I keep receiving confirmation from others
- I keep resisting this, but it returns
- None of these fit me yet
- Other (my own words)

After selecting, write one recent example (1-3 sentences).

### Q5. If you named your calling in one sentence, what would it be?

Options:
- To help people move from confusion to clarity in Christ
- To create spaces of honest healing and formation
- To teach and guide people toward faithful obedience
- To build communities where people are known and strengthened
- To serve quietly by making others' growth possible
- I am not ready to name this yet
- None of these fit me yet
- Other (my own words)

After selecting, write one recent example (1-3 sentences).

## Follow-up probes
- Which earlier answer keeps resurfacing here?
- What is the evidence for that pattern?
- What feels aligned but not yet clear?
- What would make this concrete enough to test?
- What would you stop doing if this were true?

## Move from abstraction to truth
- Force reference to prior answers.
- Ask where the pattern shows up in lived decisions.
- Ask what is repeated rather than merely admired.

## What truth sounds like
- Synthesis that names a real pattern.
- No grandiosity.
- A sentence that can be tested in life.
