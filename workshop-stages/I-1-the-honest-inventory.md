# I-1. The Honest Inventory

## Invitation
Where do I actually derive my worth right now?

## What we are trying to surface
- The sources of identity the participant actually lives from
- The gap between stated beliefs and functional trust
- The people, roles, achievements, fears, and losses that currently govern self-worth

## Questions tab format

- Present each core question with multiple-choice options where possible.
- Include `None of these fit me yet` and `Other (my own words)` in each set.
- After selection, ask for one short concrete example before moving on.

## Exercise: What remains when something is taken away?

Ask the participant to read the list below and select every line that would feel threatening if it were taken away.

Keep the first pass simple: select first, explain later.

### Exercise tab instructions

1. Select every line that feels threatening if removed.
2. Continue only after at least one line is selected.

### Visual example

- [x] My job or main role
- [ ] My ability to do something I am known for
- [x] My reputation
- [ ] My usefulness to other people
- [x] My competence or expertise
- [ ] My productivity
- [ ] My creativity or skill
- [ ] My relationships
- [ ] My status or influence
- [ ] My sense of being needed

Then ask what remains once the selected lines are removed.

## Exercise: What is true before God?

Use this as a second pass if the first exercise stays at the level of practical identity. Ask the participant to imagine every title, role, and achievement being stripped away, then ask what is still true of them before God.

Use it when the first checklist reveals strong identity attachment, but the participant has not yet named what remains apart from performance or role.

If this feels too abstract at first, return to the selected lines from the first exercise and ask what remains before God once those are gone.

### Exercise tab instructions

1. Write one or two short lines about what remains before God when titles are removed.
2. Continue once at least one line is written.

### Visual example

Even without role or achievement, I am known and loved by God.

My worth is received, not earned.

## Core questions

### Q1. When do you feel most secure in yourself?

Options:
- When I am performing well
- When people affirm me
- When I am needed by others
- When I am in control
- When I feel spiritually "on track"
- None of these fit me yet
- Other (my own words)

After selecting, write one recent example (1-3 sentences).

### Q2. What do you protect most carefully about your image?

Options:
- Competence
- Moral consistency
- Spiritual maturity
- Helpfulness
- Independence
- Composure
- None of these fit me yet
- Other (my own words)

After selecting, write one recent example (1-3 sentences).

### Q3. If your work, role, or reputation changed tomorrow, what would feel threatened?

Options:
- My sense of worth
- My place in community
- My financial safety
- My identity as useful
- My confidence
- My sense of calling
- None of these fit me yet
- Other (my own words)

After selecting, write one recent example (1-3 sentences).

### Q4. What do you reach for when you feel unseen, inadequate, or behind?

Options:
- Work harder
- Withdraw
- Seek approval
- Compare myself
- Distract myself
- Perform spiritually
- None of these fit me yet
- Other (my own words)

After selecting, write one recent example (1-3 sentences).

### Q5. Whose approval still matters too much?

Options:
- Family
- Partner or close friend
- Church leaders
- Colleagues or clients
- Social audience
- No single person, but people in general
- None of these fit me yet
- Other (my own words)

After selecting, write one recent example (1-3 sentences).

## Follow-up probes
- Tell me about a recent moment when that became obvious.
- What did you do next?
- What did you tell yourself in that moment?
- What would someone close to you notice before you would?
- If that source of worth failed, what would you fear losing?

## Move from abstraction to truth
- Ask for one recent example, not a principle.
- Ask what they do under pressure, not what they believe in theory.
- Ask what they hide.
- Ask what hurts to admit.

## What truth sounds like
- Specific, slightly uncomfortable, concrete language.
- Less self-summary, more lived evidence.
- Naming dependency without immediately defending it.
