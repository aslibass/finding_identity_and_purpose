# I-6. Repentance & Remaking

## Invitation
What am I turning from - and who am I becoming?

## What we are trying to surface
- The concrete thing the participant must release
- The form repentance actually takes in their life
- The shape of new obedience, not just regret

## Questions tab format

- Present each core question with multiple-choice options where possible.
- Include `None of these fit me yet` and `Other (my own words)` in each set.
- After selection, ask for one short concrete example before moving on.

Repentance here is response to grace already given, not a self-improvement exercise.

## Core questions

### Q1. What are you being asked to turn away from now?

Options:
- People-pleasing
- Control
- Avoidance
- Cynicism
- Hidden compromise
- Self-reliance
- None of these fit me yet
- Other (my own words)

After selecting, write one recent example (1-3 sentences).

### Q2. What pattern no longer serves the person you are becoming?

Options:
- Perfectionism
- Defensiveness
- Emotional withdrawal
- Overcommitment
- Comparison
- Procrastination
- None of these fit me yet
- Other (my own words)

After selecting, write one recent example (1-3 sentences).

### Q3. What do you need to name honestly before you can change?

Options:
- Fear
- Pride
- Resentment
- Shame
- Exhaustion
- Unbelief
- None of these fit me yet
- Other (my own words)

After selecting, write one recent example (1-3 sentences).

### Q4. What small act of obedience would prove this is real?

Options:
- Confess to someone trusted
- Apologize specifically
- Set a clear boundary
- Change one daily habit
- Make one overdue conversation happen
- Ask for prayer and accountability
- None of these fit me yet
- Other (my own words)

After selecting, write one recent example (1-3 sentences).

### Q5. What would repentance look like in your calendar, habits, or relationships?

Options:
- A weekly schedule change
- A repeated prayer rhythm
- A clear relationship repair step
- A consistent accountability check-in
- A commitment to rest and limits
- A practical stop-doing list
- None of these fit me yet
- Other (my own words)

After selecting, write one recent example (1-3 sentences).

## Follow-up probes
- What is the cost of keeping the old pattern?
- What makes release difficult?
- What are you afraid you will lose?
- What support would help you follow through?
- What would make this more than self-improvement?

## Move from abstraction to truth
- Ask for one specific thing, not a moral mood.
- Ask what behaviour must stop.
- Ask what new practice must begin.

## What truth sounds like
- Clear naming of a pattern.
- A real next step.
- No performance, only repentance that moves.
