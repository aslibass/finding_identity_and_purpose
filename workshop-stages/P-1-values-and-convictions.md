# P-1. Values & Convictions

## Invitation
What do I consistently choose when it costs me something?

## What we are trying to surface
- Values proven by action, not aspiration
- Where conviction actually shows up in trade-offs
- The difference between borrowed language and embodied belief

## Questions tab format

- Present each core question with multiple-choice options where possible.
- Include `None of these fit me yet` and `Other (my own words)` in each set.
- After selection, ask for one short concrete example before moving on.

## Exercise: Forced choice values test

Ask the participant to rank these in order of what they actually protect under pressure: comfort, truth, approval, control, peace, or loyalty. Then ask what they would sacrifice first and what they would defend hardest.

Have them number the words first, then talk through the result.

### Exercise tab instructions

1. Rank each value from 1 (most protected) to 6 (least protected).
2. Continue only after every value has a rank.

## Core questions

- Relationships

## Follow-up probes
- Tell me about the last time that value was tested.
- What did it cost you?
- What would someone close to you say you truly value?
- Where are you more consistent than you think?
- Where are you less consistent than you say?

## Move from abstraction to truth
- Ask for one decision made in the last month.
- Ask what was given up.
- Ask what was gained.

## What truth sounds like
- Recent examples.
- Cost, not slogans.
- Consistency or inconsistency named plainly.
