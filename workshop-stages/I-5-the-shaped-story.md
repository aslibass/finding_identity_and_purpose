# I-5. The Shaped Story

## Invitation
What experiences, wounds, and graces have most formed me?

## What we are trying to surface
- The participant's actual narrative arc
- The impact of suffering, failure, mercy, and formation
- Patterns that have made them resistant or available

## Questions tab format

- Present each core question with multiple-choice options where possible.
- Include `None of these fit me yet` and `Other (my own words)` in each set.
- After selection, ask for one short concrete example before moving on.

## Exercise: Draw your story as a landscape

Ask the participant to draw their story as a landscape. Mountains, valleys, rivers, deserts, roads, walls, and shelters are all valid. The aim is to surface terrain, movement, and repeated patterns without forcing polished explanation.

The point is mapping, not art; a few symbols are enough.

Treat the map as a prompt for reflection, not as a final interpretation of the participant's life.

### Exercise tab instructions

1. Sketch your story as a simple landscape.
2. Include at least three terrain features (for example: mountain, valley, river, road, wall, shelter).
3. Continue once the map has enough detail to be read.

## Core questions

### Q1. What events most changed how you see yourself or God?

Options:
- Loss or grief
- Major failure
- Unexpected success
- Relational betrayal
- Spiritual renewal
- Long season of waiting
- None of these fit me yet
- Other (my own words)

After selecting, write one recent example (1-3 sentences).

### Q2. Where have you been wounded in ways that still shape your instincts?

Options:

After selecting, write one recent example (1-3 sentences).

## Follow-up probes
- What happened before that shaped your response?
- What did you need then that you did not get?
- Where did you grow stronger, and where did you grow guarded?
- What good came out of something painful?
- What does your story explain about your present choices?

## Move from abstraction to truth
- Ask for chronological detail.
- Ask what hurt then and still hurts now.
- Ask where grace was present without them noticing.

## What truth sounds like
- Specific story fragments, not summary language.
- Wounds named without self-dramatisation.
- Gratitude and grief allowed to sit together.
